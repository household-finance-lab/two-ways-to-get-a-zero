# Literature

What was already known before this study, who established it, on what data, and what it leaves open.

---

## 1. The origin: political science, two decades ahead

**Mondak, J. J. (1999). Reconsidering the measurement of political knowledge. *Political Analysis*, 8(1), 57–82. https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.pan.a029805**

**Mondak, J. J. (2001). Developing valid knowledge scales. *American Journal of Political Science*, 45(1), 224–238. https://doi.org/10.2307/2669369**

**Mondak, J. J., & Davis, B. C. (2001). Asked and answered: Knowledge levels when we will not take "don't know" for an answer. *Political Behavior*, 23(3), 199–224. https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1015015227594**

**Mondak, J. J., & Anderson, M. R. (2004). The knowledge gap: A reexamination of gender-based differences in political knowledge. *The Journal of Politics*, 66(2), 492–512. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2508.2004.00161.x**

Respondents can be fully informed, partially informed, misinformed, or uninformed — four latent states, three response categories, and a scoring rule that collapses them into two. Two respondents with identical knowledge get different scores if they differ in willingness to answer under uncertainty.

This literature provides the theoretical foundation for distinguishing incorrect responses from “don't know” responses.

## 2. The financial translation

**Cucinelli, D., & Soana, M. G. (2023). Are financially illiterate individuals all the same? A study on incorrect and "do not know" answers to financial knowledge questions. *International Journal of Bank Marketing*, 41(4), 697–726. https://doi.org/10.1108/IJBM-06-2022-0251**

Respondents who answer incorrectly and those who answer "do not know" — both counted as illiterate under conventional scoring — differ in socio-economic characteristics and in fraud victimization risk. Bank of Italy CAPI survey, early 2020; 2,036 Italian adults; Big Five items with an explicit DK option; ordered probit and probit. Built explicitly on Mondak.

**Leaves open:** they predict *who* falls into each group. The number of correct answers is not held constant, so the incorrect-versus-DK substitution at fixed demonstrated knowledge is not estimated. Italian data, uniform item format, self-reported fraud as the outcome.

## 3. Measurement models

**Aristei, D., Bacci, S., Gallo, M., & Iannario, M. (2025). Bidimensional latent regression item response models for the assessment of financial knowledge in the presence of "Don't Know" responses. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series A*, 188(4), 1263–1298. https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssa/qnae133** (open access)

An IRTree / bidimensional 2PL: first node separates DK from a substantive answer, second separates correct from incorrect, with two latent traits plus latent regression on covariates. Italian EduFin / BVA-Doxa data, six items.

**We cannot claim novelty for treating DK as a separate dimension; Aristei et al. formally model DK and substantive responding as distinct latent processes.**

**Wilmarth, M. J., Kim, K. T., & Pak, T.-Y. (2023). What do we really know about "don't know"? Re-assessing the measurement of financial knowledge. *Journal of Consumer Affairs*, 57(4), 1623–1649. https://doi.org/10.1111/joca.12563**

DK responding is associated with personality and emotional factors, reinforcing that DK should not be interpreted as a clean measure of knowledge, confidence, or uncertainty recognition.

**Pearson, B., Korankye, T., Liu, Y., & Qing, D. (2024). Knowing the "don't knows" to financial literacy questions in the U.S. National Financial Capability Study. *Journal of Financial Counseling and Planning*, 35(2), 292–304. https://doi.org/10.1891/JFCP-2023-0067**

NFCS — the nearest neighbor by dataset.

**Kim, N., & Mountain, T. P. (2019). Financial knowledge and "don't know" response. *Journal of Consumer Affairs*, 53(4), 1948–1969. https://doi.org/10.1111/joca.12275**

Proposed replacing DK using sample proportions; showed the gender gap in objective knowledge is almost entirely attributable to the gender differential in DK propensity.

**Bazley, W. J., Bonaparte, Y., & Korniotis, G. M. (2021). Financial self-awareness: Who knows what they don't know? *Finance Research Letters*, 38, 101445.** *(Published version, not the SSRN working paper.)*

**Bertola, G., & Lo Prete, A. (2025). Who prefers guessing to admitting they don't know? Measurement error in financial literacy surveys. *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 233, 107003.**

Structural model of guessing using two waves of Italian financial-literacy data.

**Knoll, M. A. Z., & Houts, C. R. (2012). The financial knowledge scale: An application of item response theory to the assessment of financial literacy. *Journal of Consumer Affairs*, 46(3), 381–410. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6606.2012.01241.x**

Item response theory applied to financial knowledge items.

**Krosnick, J. A., & Presser, S. (2010). Question and questionnaire design. In P. V. Marsden & J. D. Wright (Eds.), *Handbook of Survey Research* (2nd ed., pp. 263–313). Emerald.**

Satisficing. Offering DK lets the uninformed admit it, and also discourages the partially informed from making the effort.

## 4. Experimental evidence on the DK option

**Tranfaglia, A., Lloro, A., & Merry, E. (2024). Question design and the gender gap in financial literacy. FEDS Notes 2024-01-02, Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System. https://doi.org/10.17016/2380-7172.3415** *(A FEDS Note, not a peer-reviewed article.)*

Removing the DK option raises measured knowledge, and part of that increase is guessing rather than knowledge. 2021 SHED, n ≈ 11,000, between-subject randomization.

**Relevance to this study:** the Fed notes that a larger conversion share is expected on an item with only two answer choices. That is federal-agency precedent for treating guess probability as an item property — the argument behind our item audit and behind the experiment we are designing.

**Bucher-Koenen, T., Alessie, R., Lusardi, A., & van Rooij, M. (2025). Fearless woman: Financial literacy, confidence, and stock market participation. _Management Science_, 71(9), 7414–7430. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.00425**

Dutch panel; within-subject, the same respondents six weeks apart.

## 5. FINRA's own work

**Urban, C., & Valdes, O. (2022). *Why Is Measured Financial Literacy Declining and What Does It Mean? Maybe We Just "Don't Know."* FINRA Foundation Insights, August 2022.**

Across five NFCS waves (2009–2021), mean correct fell from 2.99 to 2.58 out of 5 while mean DK rose from 1.22 to 1.45. **The rise in DK accounts for roughly 75% of the drop in measured financial knowledge.** Excluding DK from incorrect, the incorrect count still rose, so the decline holds among those who attempt. FINRA states the causes are unclear.

**Scope:** this covers the five-item Big Five on the State-by-State survey, not the eleven-item Investor Survey quiz we use.

Also from FINRA's 2024 Investor Survey report: investors who rate their own knowledge highly answer more questions incorrectly, and 51% of them accept the guaranteed-return proposition. This establishes that knowledge, self-assessed knowledge, and judgment can diverge within the Investor Survey, providing important context for our analysis of incorrect and DK responses.

## 6. What we claim, and what we do not

**We do not claim:**

- to have discovered that DK and incorrect responses differ — Mondak (1999–2004); Cucinelli & Soana (2023)
- to have first modeled DK as a separate dimension — Aristei et al. (2025)
- to have first shown that removing DK inflates measured knowledge — Tranfaglia et al. (2024); Bucher-Koenen et al. (2025)
- to have first documented gendered DK propensity — Kim & Mountain (2019), and a large literature behind it
- that DK measures confidence, calibration, uncertainty recognition, or metacognition — we have no independent measure of any of these

**What appears to remain open:**

1. **The instrument.** The U.S. studies identified in this review primarily use the Big Three or Big Five; we did not identify prior work applying this approach to the NFCS Investor Survey's eleven-item quiz.
2. **Format heterogeneity.** Two to five answer options across items, so guess probability ranges from .20 to .50, and DK rates reach 40%. The Big Five lacks this variation. Two items are answered *below chance* by those who attempt them, which we believe is undocumented for this instrument.
3. **Judgment in the same instrument.** These respondents are investors making real decisions, and the same survey carries a behavioral judgment item (G43).
4. **Holding demonstrated knowledge constant.** The incorrect-versus-DK substitution at a fixed number correct is, as far as we can tell, not estimated elsewhere.
